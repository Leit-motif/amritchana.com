Research: keeping agent work legible

I'm Amrit Chana, an independent AI researcher in Chicago. I study how a person can stay in charge of AI agents whose work has grown past what anyone can read.

The problem

Agents now run for days across hundreds of sessions. The person they work for can't read it all, so they rely on summaries, and a summary can drop a commitment, miss a reversal, or credit the wrong party without anyone noticing.

What I've built

Vantage is the instrument I use to study this. It shows a live multi-agent system's sessions, decisions, claims and commitments as a graph on my phone and tablet, with realtime voice. Two rules make it a research tool rather than a dashboard:

  • Every summary opens onto its exact source. The interface may compress; it may never misrepresent. Any error can be checked against the original record.
  • I change things only by proposal. Edits are recorded with who proposed them; the original record is kept.

I've used it daily since late October 2026, on a system that has recorded 1,942 agent sessions.

Current questions

  1. Summary accuracy. How often do small models omit or distort facts that matter (commitments, reversals, who did what) compared with larger models?
  2. Attention. Can a small model decide what deserves my attention, and when to interrupt me, as well as my own recorded rulings do?
  3. Judges. When model judges agree with each other, how often do I disagree with both?

What I'll publish

  • A report comparing small and large models on summary accuracy and attention judgments.
  • A rubric and synthetic test cases for checking agent-session summaries against their sources (no personal data).
  • Open data specifications for building this kind of interface on other agent systems.
