# Visual research: AI consulting that feels worth paying for

**Reviewed 2026-10-09.** I searched and opened the real service/case-study pages below; visually inspected browser renders for Deloitte, BCG, Accenture, PwC, Cloudworks, and Pacific AI Tech. Text was also read from the page bodies. McKinsey's QuantumBlack URL was found, but McKinsey blocked both text fetch (403) and browser rendering in this session. Boutique examples are a deliberately small scan, not a ranking: ownership/quality isn't independently verified and every metric on their pages is provider-reported unless stated otherwise.

## What repeats across the big firms

1. **Sell a business outcome, not a model.** Deloitte frames readiness → acceleration → advantage; Accenture organizes the offer around productivity, growth, risk, experience, and knowledge; PwC says goal-oriented AI and targeted business needs. The visual hierarchy is a clear business headline and scannable capability blocks, not a technical diagram as the entire pitch.
2. **Proof is specific and layered.** BCG links to impact stories, executive survey findings, products and named experts; Deloitte layers analyst/partner recognition; PwC uses expert/industry quotes and its outcome-pricing claims. Client stories, named people, third-party recognition, and research are more credible than a large claim with no source.
3. **People and real work are common.** PwC's live page leads with a large crop of colleagues in a work conversation. Deloitte's current hero uses an abstract, colorful 3D folded-ribbon form; BCG mixes a restrained white/green editorial hero with a dark technology image just below. The imagery is art-directed and integrated, not a lonely stock photo floating beside a generic AI headline.
4. **Typography/color are deliberate, not one universal 'AI palette'.** BCG uses dark-green serif display, black sans body, white space and an outlined category pill; PwC pairs black editorial serif with white and its orange brand; Accenture uses black/white, oversized sans and a strong violet strip; Deloitte's hero is black with colored 3D art. The invariant is confident type scale, disciplined spacing, and one coherent palette—not blue particles.
5. **Premium comes from restraint + legibility + receipts.** Pages make scope, governance, delivery and trust visible alongside innovation. Signals that feel cheap are clichéd AI stock, fake client marks, unsupported percent claims, tiny low-contrast copy, and dashboard-shaped decoration with no actual work attached.

## Small / independent consultancy scan

- [Cloudworks — Thornbury Logistics](https://cloudworks.co.uk/case-studies/ai-consultancy): straightforward case-study hierarchy (business, challenge, solution, steps); actual workflow is inbox/website → CRM/spreadsheet → draft quote, retaining human review. The provider says names were changed and offers a reference call. Hero is mostly typography/white space with a soft teal/blue gradient; case body stays practical.
- [Pacific AI Tech — case studies](https://www.pacificaitech.com/case-studies): warm ivory/ink/forest-green, editorial serif headline, monospaced navigation, and named small-business use cases; page explicitly shows problem → build → handoff → operating change and human control. More grounded than a generic AI explainer.
- [Nexus AI — small-business case studies](https://nexusaiconcepts.com/case-studies/): disclosed founder relationships; includes a four-person landscaping company and an actual quote-drafting workflow, named tools, before/after timing, a specific owner quote. Their own disclosure is a useful proof pattern; don't copy unverified numbers.
- [Ogentic — professional services case studies](https://www.ogentic.co.uk/case-studies): presents workflow narrative, existing tools and quantified claimed outcomes. Its images are generic Unsplash business/meeting photos; illustrates how stock photography alone doesn't create trust.
- [Covenant AI — Covenant Sites case study](https://covenantai.consulting/case-study): a service-business workflow is made legible through a before/after task table and human review steps; importantly, it is presented as the provider's own business, not a client testimonial.

## Design implication for this hero

After the forest resolves, make the third beat a warm, calm workroom with morning forest light carried through the window. Put an original, clearly labelled **EXAMPLE** workflow UI on the laptop: a small business inbox feeding an invoice/quote review queue, with a human approval state and a plain outcome line such as “Routine follow-up, drafted. You decide what goes out.” Avoid fake company names, logos, metrics, and stock photography. That is the trustable middle: familiar real work plus visible human control, not fake enterprise theatre.
